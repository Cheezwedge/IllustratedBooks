# 1 · Rights, territories and what you may sell where

> **Not legal advice.** This is a planning summary compiled in November 2026 to help you ask the right questions. Copyright terms change, and the Tolkien Estate, HarperCollins and Middle-earth Enterprises enforce their rights actively. Before you print a single copy, have an intellectual-property lawyer **in each country you plan to sell in** confirm the points marked ⚖️.

## 1.1 The short version

| Question | Answer (as of Nov 2026) |
|---|---|
| When did Tolkien die? | 2 September 1973 |
| Where is *The Hobbit* public domain? | Countries with a **life + 50 years** term that had not extended it before 1 January 2024. **New Zealand** is the clearest English-language example; Tolkien's works entered the public domain there on 1 Jan 2024. ⚖️ |
| Where is it **not** public domain? | The **US** (the 1937 text until 1 Jan 2033; the revised 1951 and 1966 texts much later), the **UK, EU, Australia, Canada, Japan, South Korea, Singapore, Russia** and every other life + 70 country, until **1 Jan 2044**. India and other life + 60 countries: until **1 Jan 2034**. |
| Is copyright the only issue? | No. **Trademarks** (Middle-earth Enterprises owns marks such as THE HOBBIT and character names in many countries) and the **look of the films** are separate rights that do not expire with the book's copyright. |
| Can I use the Brambly Hedge style? | A general *style* (pen and watercolour, cutaways, cosy hedgerow detail) is not protected. Specific characters, compositions, the *Brambly Hedge* name and its branding are. Make it your own, and never use the name or Jill Barklem's in your title, marketing, keywords or AI prompts. |

## 1.2 Life + 50 countries to investigate

These countries are commonly listed with a life + 50 term. Each one needs checking for later amendments, rules about foreign works, and whether you can realistically print and distribute there. ⚖️

- **New Zealand**: best first market. English-speaking, life + 50, Tolkien public domain since 2024. NZ committed to life + 70 under its free-trade agreements with the UK and EU, and legislation is reportedly planned for around 2028. Reports say the extension will **not revive** works already in the public domain, but **confirm this before you invest**.
- **South Africa**: English-speaking and life + 50 for literary works. A long-running Copyright Amendment Bill has been in progress, so check its status.
- Others often cited: Egypt, mainland China, Hong Kong, Taiwan, Malaysia, Thailand, the Philippines, Pakistan, Iran, Kenya, Uganda, Zambia, Zimbabwe and Botswana. Some are realistic only for translated editions.

**Not on the list:** Canada extended to life + 70 on 30 Dec 2022, before Tolkien's term ran out, so it is protected there until 2044.

## 1.3 Which text to use

| Edition | Notes | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| **1937** first edition (George Allen & Unwin) | The true "original". Chapter 5 tells the riddle-game ending differently. | Lowest |
| **1951** second edition | Tolkien revised Chapter 5 to fit *The Lord of the Rings*. | Low in NZ (same author and term) |
| **1966** third edition | Tolkien's last revision and the familiar modern text. | Low in NZ |
| Post-1973 printings | May contain editorial corrections, new forewords, notes, or maps redrawn by others. | **Avoid**: later editorial material can carry its own copyright |

Rules of thumb:

- **Transcribe from a printing made before 1974**, ideally a 1937–1966 copy you own or can borrow, or a properly sourced public-domain transcription in your territory.
- **Do not scan or OCR a modern edition.** NZ protects the *typographical arrangement* of a published edition for 25 years, so the page layout of a recent HarperCollins printing is off-limits even where the words are free. ⚖️
- Leave out modern introductions, notes, annotated-edition material and any "Note on the Text" written after 1973.
- Tolkien's own drawings and maps for *The Hobbit* are his works too, so the same term applies. The aim of this edition, though, is **all-new art**, so draw new maps rather than tracing his.

## 1.4 Trademarks and look-alike risk ⚖️

- **Middle-earth Enterprises** (owned by Embracer Group since 2022) holds registrations for names including THE HOBBIT and BILBO BAGGINS in many classes and countries, including printed matter. Search the registers before launch: **IPONZ** (New Zealand) and **CIPC** (South Africa).
- Using a book's real title to describe that book is usually a descriptive, non-trademark use. You strengthen that position by:
  - using the full traditional title: *The Hobbit, or There and Back Again*;
  - setting the title in your own lettering, never the films' logo or typeface;
  - leaving out "Official", "Authorised" and the Tolkien Estate's **JRRT monogram**, which is itself a registered mark;
  - carrying a clear disclaimer (wording is in the copyright-page template).
- **Avoid film resemblance.** The 2012–2014 films and the 1977 animated film are separate copyrighted works. Do not echo their character faces, costumes, set designs (the film Bag End, Erebor, Lake-town), creature designs (the film's Smaug walks on his wings), invented characters, or posters.
- **Other illustrators.** Do not copy compositions from any published *Hobbit* illustrator: Tolkien himself, Alan Lee, John Howe, Jemima Catlin, Michael Hague, Tove Jansson, the Rankin/Bass designers, and others.

## 1.5 Selling: the practical traps

1. **Where the copy is made matters.** Printing a copy is an act of reproduction in the country of printing. A print-on-demand order from a New Zealand customer may be **printed in Australia**, where the book is in copyright until 2044. Use an **NZ-based printer** (or another public-domain territory), and ship only within public-domain territories. ⚖️
2. **Set territories on every platform.** Restrict each digital store to your cleared countries. Expect platforms to ask for proof of public-domain status, and some may refuse the title anyway.
3. **Amazon KDP** has specific public-domain rules:
   - Your edition must be *differentiated*. "Illustrated" requires **10 or more original illustrations** relevant to the book, and this plan has about 200.
   - The title field must carry the tag, e.g. *The Hobbit (Illustrated)*.
   - Public-domain titles are restricted from KDP Select and some royalty options.
   - You must declare AI-generated content if you use any.
4. **Your own web shop** should geo-restrict both downloads and shipping addresses.
5. **Mark the book.** Put "For sale only in New Zealand [and other cleared territories]" on the copyright page and the back cover.
6. **Expand later.** The book enters the public domain in India and other life + 60 countries in **2034**, in the **US in 2033** (1937 text only), and in the **UK, EU and Australia in 2044**. Make sure your illustration contracts already cover those territories (see the art bible §2.9).

## 1.6 New Zealand housekeeping

- **ISBNs** are issued **free** by the National Library of New Zealand's ISBN Agency. Use one per format: hardback, paperback, EPUB and PDF.
- **Legal deposit:** NZ publishers must deposit copies of new publications, print and electronic, with the National Library.
- If you trade as a business, register for GST once you pass the turnover threshold.

## 1.7 Who owns *your* edition

- **Your illustrations, design, typography, introduction and notes** are new copyright works, and they are what you own and sell.
- **AI-generated art:** in the US, purely AI-generated images cannot be registered for copyright. NZ's Act has a "computer-generated works" provision that may protect them, but this is untested here. Art you create yourself, or substantially rework by hand, is much easier to protect and to defend as "original illustrations". Keep your layered working files as evidence.
